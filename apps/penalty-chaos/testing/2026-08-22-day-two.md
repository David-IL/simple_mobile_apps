# Playtest 2 — 2026-08-22

**Who:** David's son (11).
**What:** he asked, that morning, unprompted, whether he could play it again.
**Context:** the graphics changes from the evening before had landed on the phone.

## The headline

**He asked for it himself.** Nobody mentioned it, suggested it or left it open on
the screen.

That is the first half of [§6's](../../../docs/research/penalty-chaos.md) actual
success bar — "plays it unprompted a week later". One day is not a week, and
this should be recorded as *day one of that measurement*, not as the measurement
being passed. But the property that matters is the unprompted part, and that is
now observed rather than hoped for.

Worth noting what it is **not** evidence of: nothing here says anything about
whether a child who is not the builder's son, in a house where this is not the
new thing, would do the same.

## The finding that changes the framing

Unprompted commentary, in his own words:

> "X has become much better." · "Ahh, Y is bad."

He is talking about the keepers as **characters with reputations**, by the names
he gave them, and tracking how they behave across matches. Not about penalties,
not about the chaos, not about winning.

That is the clearest signal from either session, and it settles a question the
research doc left open.

### The research doc predicted this and the playtests have now confirmed it

[§9](../../../docs/research/penalty-chaos.md) set three conditions on proceeding.
The second was:

> **The keeper is the star, not the weather.** The one viral proof point in this
> exact space — *La Vieja contra el Dibu* — is built entirely on a taunting
> keeper who reads your pattern, with no random disruptions at all. Build that
> first. Wind and pitch invaders are garnish on top of a mechanic that has to be
> fun without them.

Two sessions in:

- The keepers are what he talks about, unprompted, by name.
- Of five disruptions, two worked, two failed, and one had to be deleted.

The research called it, and the evidence agrees. **The disruptions are texture.
The keepers are the product.** §3's "riskiest assumption" — that comedic
disruptions are the hook — should now be read as answered: they are not.

### Renaming is doing more work than expected

It was justified on "specific beats generic" grounds — let a player put a local
name on a keeper. What it actually produced is **ownership**. He is not saying
"the hard one", he is saying a name he chose, about a character he has opinions
about. That is a stronger effect than the feature was designed for, and it is
free of the rights problem that shipping real names would have carried
([ADR 8](../../../docs/adr/0008-no-real-person-likenesses-or-club-ip.md)).

### The mud fix was noticed without prompting

> "The muddy field looks much better now."

The change was making the effect visible across the whole pitch instead of a
splat by the spot. A spontaneous positive reaction to exactly the thing that was
changed is direct confirmation of the legibility diagnosis from
[playtest 1](2026-08-21-first-playtest.md): a gag lands when its *effect* is
visible, not when its presence is.

## Reading this carefully

- **One child, one day, and he is the builder's son.** Novelty and wanting to
  please are both live.
- **"X has become much better" is ambiguous.** It could mean the keeper's
  pattern-reading is being felt as an opponent that adapts — the core mechanic
  landing — or simply commentary on how a match is going. Worth listening for
  which, because they mean very different things.
- **Do not over-rotate.** Two sessions is not a trend, and the temptation after a
  good morning is to rebuild the whole app around one remark.

## What this implies

1. **Stop investing in disruptions.** Two work, two are weak, one is gone. They
   are texture and should stay cheap.
2. **Keeper personality is where effort pays.** More distinct behaviour, more
   distinct faces, more to have an opinion about.
3. **Feed the thing he is already doing.** He is tracking keepers as opponents
   with records. The app currently remembers nothing about them between matches.
   A per-keeper tally — how many you have scored past each one — would make that
   real rather than something he holds in his head.
4. **Still do nothing about the art in general.** Only the mud comment was about
   graphics, and it was about a *legibility* change, not polish.

## Open

- The week-later measurement is still running. Do not declare §6 passed.
- Watch whether he notices the keeper reading his pattern, and whether he adapts
  to it deliberately. That is the mechanic the whole design rests on and there is
  still no direct evidence it is understood.
